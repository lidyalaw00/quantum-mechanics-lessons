Why do ladder operators look like that?

We know what creation and annihilation operators do. But why must they be built from this particular combination of position and momentum? Start with the physics, and let the mathematics follow.

01 · The physical goal

Energy comes in steps.

We know that the energy levels of the quantum harmonic oscillator are discrete and equally spaced, like a ladder.

The difference between two adjacent energy levels is exactly one quantum of energy, ℏω.

En=ℏω(n+12),n=0,1,2,…En+1−En=ℏω

So it is natural to ask: can we construct an operator that moves us up by exactly one step, and another that moves us down by exactly one step?

We call the operator that adds one quantum the creation operator, â†, and the one that removes one quantum the annihilation operator, â. They should take an energy eigenstate to the next or previous eigenstate, up to a multiplicative factor.

The puzzle: how do we turn this simple physical idea into an actual mathematical expression involving position and momentum?

02 · Translate the physics into mathematics

What must an annihilation operator satisfy?

Start from what we want the operator to do: remove exactly one quantum of energy.

The Hamiltonian Ĥ measures energy in the sense that an energy eigenstate satisfies

H^|n⟩=En|n⟩

After applying â, the new vector â|n⟩, if nonzero, should have an energy lower by one quantum.

What should happen when Ĥ acts on â|n⟩?

Think about the energy of the resulting state, not just the amount of energy that was removed.

Answer correctly to reveal the next part.

This gives our desired equation:

Ĥâ|n⟩ = (En − ℏω)â|n⟩
= âĤ|n⟩ − ℏω â|n⟩,
(Ĥâ − âĤ)|n⟩ = −ℏω â|n⟩.

Since we want this to hold for every energy eigenstate, the corresponding operator relation (on their span) is

The requirement for lowering energy
[Ĥ, â] = −ℏω â.

Here [Ĥ,â] = Ĥâ − âĤ. The same reasoning for adding energy gives

[Ĥ, â†] = +ℏω â†.

In other words, the commutator with the Hamiltonian must return the same operator multiplied by the energy change. This is an eigenvalue problem for operators. Notice that we have not yet assumed any formula for â.

03 · Look for the ingredients

Why not just position or momentum?

For the harmonic oscillator, the natural ingredients are position and momentum, because both appear in its Hamiltonian.

H^=p^22m+12mω2x^2
[x̂, p̂] = iℏ.

Let us test whether position or momentum alone can do the job. Using the canonical commutation relation, we find

[H^,x^]=−iℏmp^[H^,p^]=iℏmω2x^

[Ĥ,x̂] = [p̂²,x̂]/(2m) = −iℏp̂/m.

= 2iℏx̂.

x̂→p̂

p̂→x̂

Neither x̂ nor p̂ alone satisfies the desired commutator. But they transform into each other. That is a very useful clue: perhaps a suitable linear combination of the two can transform back into itself.

04 · Find the right combination

Let the condition choose the formula.

Try the simplest possibility: a linear combination of position and momentum.

â = C(x̂ + αp̂).

We do not know α or C yet. For now, the overall scale C cancels out of the commutator condition (assuming C is nonzero). Substitute this trial operator into [Ĥ,â] = −ℏωâ:

[Ĥ,x̂] + α[Ĥ,p̂] = −ℏω(x̂ + αp̂).

Using the commutators we just calculated, we obtain

−iℏmp^+αiℏmω2x^=−ℏωx^−ℏωαp^

The coefficients of x̂ must match on both sides, and the coefficients of p̂ must match as well.

From the p̂ terms, −iℏ/m = −ℏωα. What is α?

Divide both sides by the coefficient multiplying α, keeping track of the two minus signs.

Answer correctly to reveal the next part.

−iℏm=−ℏωα⟹α=imω

This also satisfies the x̂ coefficient: αiℏmω² = −ℏω. Therefore, up to an overall constant,

a^∝x^+ip^mω

Similarly, we can find the appropriate linear combination of x^ and p^ for the creation operator by requiring it to add one quantum of energy:

a^†∝x^−ip^mω

The imaginary unit i and the factor 1/(mω) are not arbitrary. They are forced by the requirement that this linear combination remove one energy quantum.

05 · Choose the scale

Why the square root in front?

The energy shift fixes the relative weights of x̂ and p̂, but not the overall size of the operator.

We have found (choosing C real and positive)

a^=C(x^+ip^mω)a^†=C(x^−ip^mω)

We choose the standard normalization [â,â†] = 1. It makes the operators dimensionless and gives the usual occupation-number algebra. Multiplying by any nonzero constant would still shift the energy by one quantum, but would change the amplitudes.

[â,â†] = C²(−2i/(mω))[x̂,p̂]
= C²(2ℏ/(mω)) = 1.

Thus

C=mω2ℏ

Combining this with our earlier result gives the familiar formulas:

Annihilation operator
a^=mω2ℏ(x^+ip^mω)

Similarly, we can find the linear combination of x^ and p^ for the creation operator by requiring it to add one quantum of energy.

Show the derivation for the creation operator

We want the creation operator to increase the energy by ℏω, so it must satisfy

[H^,a^†]=ℏωa^†

Try the same linear combination, but with an unknown coefficient β:

a^†=C(x^+βp^)

Substitute this into the commutator condition and cancel the nonzero constant C.

[H^,x^]+β[H^,p^]=ℏω(x^+βp^)

Using the same commutators as before, we get

−iℏmp^+βiℏmω2x^=ℏωx^+ℏωβp^

Compare the coefficients of p^ on both sides:

−iℏm=ℏωβ⟹β=−imω

This also satisfies the coefficient of x^:

βiℏmω2=(−imω)iℏmω2=ℏω

Thus the momentum term has the opposite sign to that of the annihilation operator. Since the two operators are Hermitian adjoints, they have the same real, positive normalization constant:

C=mω2ℏ
Creation operator
a^†=mω2ℏ(x^−ip^mω)
06 · Put it all together

From a formula to a physical meaning.

Now the operator definitions no longer look mysterious. We can also recover the familiar number-state rules.

Multiply â† and â, keeping in mind that x̂ and p̂ do not commute:

H^=ℏω(a^†a^+12)

What happens if we try to remove a quantum from the ground state?

Remove one quantum

For n ≥ 1; â|0⟩ = 0.

Add one quantum

a^†|n⟩=n+1|n+1⟩

For every n ≥ 0.

Where do √n and √(n+1) come from?

Since â lowers the energy, write â|n⟩ = cn|n−1⟩ for n ≥ 1. Then

|cn|2=⟨n|a^†a^|n⟩=n⟹cn=n

Similarly, ââ† = â†â + 1, so

∥a^†|n⟩∥2=⟨n|a^a^†|n⟩=n+1⟹a^†|n⟩=n+1|n+1⟩

We choose the relative phases of the number states so that both ladder coefficients are real and nonnegative.

The ground state still has zero-point energy ℏω/2, but there is no lower energy eigenstate. The annihilation operator kills the ground state; its output is the zero vector, not another normalized state.

We do not guess a strange formula and then hope it lowers the energy. We decide what the operator should do, and its mathematical form follows from that requirement within the simplest linear ansatz.
