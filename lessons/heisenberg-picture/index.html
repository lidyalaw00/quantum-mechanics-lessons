01Start with time evolution

In quantum mechanics, the Hamiltonian H governs how the state changes with time. We describe this using the Schrödinger equation:

iℏddt|ψ(t)⟩=H|ψ(t)⟩

For a time-independent Hamiltonian, solving this equation gives

|ψ(t)⟩=U(t)|ψ(0)⟩U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ

The unitary U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ is called the time evolution operator. It takes the state at time zero to the state at time t.

02The same physics in two pictures

What we can observe experimentally are measurement outcomes and their probabilities. For example, suppose we want the expectation value of an observable A at time t:

⟨A⟩t=⟨ψ(t)|A|ψ(t)⟩

Substitute the time-evolved ket and its corresponding bra:

|ψ(t)⟩=U(t)|ψ(0)⟩,⟨ψ(t)|=⟨ψ(0)|U†(t)
⟨A⟩t=⟨ψ(0)|U†(t)AU(t)|ψ(0)⟩

Here is the key idea: we can group exactly the same expression in two different ways. We can absorb the time evolution into the state, or into the observable.

03Derive the Heisenberg equation of motion

In the Schrödinger picture, the Schrödinger equation describes how the state evolves. We want the corresponding equation for an operator in the Heisenberg picture.

Step 1 · Write the evolving operator

Use the definition we just obtained, with A the fixed Schrödinger-picture observable:

A(t)=U†(t)AU(t)=eiHt/ℏAe−iHt/ℏ

Step 2 · Differentiate using the product rule

Only the exponentials depend on time. The derivative can therefore act on either exponential:

dA(t)dt=iHℏeiHt/ℏAe−iHt/ℏ⏟A(t)+eiHt/ℏAe−iHt/ℏ⏟A(t)(−iHℏ)

We may move H past its own exponential because they commute, but we cannot assume H commutes with A.

Step 3 · Recognize the commutator

Both exponential sandwiches are just A(t). Thus

dA(t)dt=iℏHA(t)−iℏA(t)H=1iℏ(A(t)H−HA(t))

Recall that [A,H]=AH−HA. Therefore,

dA(t)dt=1iℏ[A(t),H]

This is the Heisenberg equation of motion. The Hamiltonian generates time evolution, and the commutator determines how the observable responds to that evolution.

04Apply it to the harmonic oscillator

Now let the operator be the annihilation operator a. For a harmonic oscillator,

H=ℏω(a†a+12),[a,a†]=1

All we need to find is the commutator of the annihilation operator with the Hamiltonian.

Show the commutator calculation
[a,H]=ℏω[a,a†a]=ℏω([a,a†]a+a†[a,a])=ℏωa

We used [X,YZ] = [X,Y]Z + Y[X,Z], [a,a†] = 1 and [a,a] = 0. The constant ½ commutes with every operator.

Since [a,H]=ℏωa, substitute into the Heisenberg equation:

da(t)dt=1iℏ[a(t),H]=−iωa(t)

This is a simple differential equation. Its solution is

a(t)=a(0)e−iωt

Similarly, by taking the adjoint, the creation operator evolves as a†(t)=a†(0)eiωt. Both acquire a time-dependent phase at the natural oscillator frequency.

Takeaway: Time evolution can be assigned to either the state or the observable. The Heisenberg equation follows by differentiating the observable's unitary transformation. Both pictures describe exactly the same physics.
