Can a Gaussian oscillate without spreading?

The ground state has minimum uncertainty. If we shift its Gaussian and release it, will it oscillate without spreading? Let’s use ladder operators to find out.

Step 01

Definite energy is not definite position

Start with the ground state |0⟩. The label 0 refers to its energy level, not its position.

Ĥ |0⟩ = (ℏω/2) |0⟩

Its energy is definite, but position and momentum are uncertain. Both distributions are Gaussians centered at zero. Being centered at zero means the average is zero, not that every measurement gives zero.

For an energy eigenstate |n⟩,

σxσp = ℏ(n + ½)
Derive the uncertainty product

Using x̂ = √(ℏ/(2mω))(â + â†) and p̂ = i√(mℏω/2)(â† − â), the means vanish in |n⟩. The second moments give

σx² = ℏ(2n+1)/(2mω)
σp² = mℏω(2n+1)/2

Multiplying the standard deviations gives ℏ(n + ½).

Step 03

Meet the coherent state

A ground-state Gaussian displaced in position, momentum, or both is called a coherent state. Recall that â|0⟩ = 0. The displaced state satisfies

â |α⟩ = α |α⟩

This is the definition of a coherent state: applying the annihilation operator returns the same state, multiplied by a complex number α (alpha). The ground state is the special case α = 0.

What does α tell us?

From the lecture, â is a combination of position and momentum. Since ⟨â⟩ = α,

α = √(mω/(2ℏ)) (⟨x̂⟩ + i⟨p̂⟩/(mω))

The real part of α gives the average position, and its imaginary part gives the average momentum.

Is it an energy eigenstate?

No (unless α = 0). Shifting is not the same as applying â† once, which gives |1⟩. A coherent state is a particular superposition of energy eigenstates:

|α⟩ = e−|α|²/2 ∑n=0∞ (αn/√(n!)) |n⟩

Derive this superposition

Step 04

Why does the center oscillate?

Since α tells us where the packet is centered, we only need to find how α evolves. Using Ĥ = ℏω(â†â + ½) and [â, â†] = 1, the equation of motion gives

d⟨â⟩/dt = ⟨[â, Ĥ]⟩/(iℏ) = −iω⟨â⟩

The state stays coherent (see the derivation below), so ⟨â⟩ = α at every time. Hence

α(t) = α₀e−iωt = α₀[cos(ωt) − i sin(ωt)]

Its real and imaginary parts oscillate. Using the relation between α, position and momentum,

⟨x̂⟩t = x₀ cos(ωt)
⟨p̂⟩t = −mωx₀ sin(ωt)

The center moves just like a classical harmonic oscillator!

Why does the state stay coherent?

In the expansion of |α₀⟩, each |n⟩ acquires the phase e−iωt(n+½). This simply replaces α₀ by α₀e−iωt in the coefficients, with an overall phase e−iωt/2. The energy probabilities stay unchanged because the phases have unit magnitude.

Step 05

Why does the uncertainty stay at ℏ/2?

The center changes as α evolves. But does the width change? We can find out using the same relation â|α⟩ = α|α⟩.

For position, use x̂ = √(ℏ/(2mω))(â + â†). The coherent-state relation and [â, â†] = 1 give

⟨x̂⟩² = (ℏ/(2mω))(α + α*)²
⟨x̂²⟩ = (ℏ/(2mω))[(α + α*)² + 1]

Subtract the squared mean from the second moment. All the α-dependent terms cancel! Doing the same for momentum gives

σx² = ⟨x̂²⟩ − ⟨x̂⟩² = ℏ/(2mω)
σp² = ⟨p̂²⟩ − ⟨p̂⟩² = mℏω/2
σxσp = ℏ/2

α determines the moving center, not the width. Neither uncertainty depends on α, so the product remains ℏ/2 at all times.

See the ladder-operator calculation

From â|α⟩ = α|α⟩, we obtain

⟨â⟩ = α,   ⟨â†⟩ = α*
⟨â²⟩ = α²,   ⟨(â†)²⟩ = (α*)²
⟨â†â⟩ = |α|²,   ⟨ââ†⟩ = |α|² + 1

Substituting these into x̂ gives the position moments above. For p̂ = i√(mℏω/2)(â† − â),

⟨p̂⟩² = (mℏω/2)[−(α* − α)²]
⟨p̂²⟩ = (mℏω/2)[−(α* − α)² + 1]

Subtracting gives the stated momentum variance.

CONCEPT CHECK 06

Does a minimum uncertainty product necessarily mean the oscillator is in the ground state?

Lesson complete

Yes — the center oscillates, and the uncertainty stays minimal.

Only the ground state minimizes σₓσₚ among energy eigenstates. A displaced ground-state Gaussian is a coherent state, which is a particular superposition of energy eigenstates. Its relative phases evolve so that its center follows classical motion while its position and momentum widths remain unchanged.

σxσp = ℏ/2   at all times
