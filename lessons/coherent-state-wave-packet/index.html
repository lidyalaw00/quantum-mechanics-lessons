A student question

Can a minimum-uncertainty packet move?

Shift the ground-state Gaussian in a harmonic trap. It is no longer an energy eigenstate, but it still has the same initial shape. What happens next?

The short answer

Yes. Its center oscillates.

The packet moves back and forth without changing its width. Its mean position and mean momentum oscillate, while the uncertainty product stays at σxσp = ℏ/2 at every time. This moving state is called a coherent state.

The uncertainty tells us how spread out the packet is around its center. Moving that center does not, by itself, increase the spread.

The ladder-operator picture

Why is a shifted packet not an energy eigenstate?

The ground state obeys a|0⟩=0 and is centered at the bottom of the trap. A shifted Gaussian has the same width, but its mean position is d≠0. An excited energy eigenstate |n⟩ does not simply look like a displaced ground-state Gaussian.

In ladder-operator language, shifting the ground state produces a superposition of many energy levels:

|α⟩ = e−|α|²/2 ∑n=0∞ αn/√(n!) |n⟩,   α = d/(2σx)

Here σx=√(ℏ/2mω). At the start, α is real because we shifted position without giving the packet a momentum kick. The bars show the probabilities for the current shift.

Which energy levels are present?

The bars stay fixed as the packet moves: the Hamiltonian does not change the probability of each energy eigenstate. It changes their relative phases.

Each |n⟩ gains a phase at its own energy En=(n+½)ℏω. Together, those phases move the center. Equivalently, a coherent state obeys a|α⟩=α|α⟩, and its label rotates as α(t)=αe−iωt. The real and imaginary parts give the moving mean position and momentum.

The uncertainty question

How can it move and still reach ℏ/2?

The ground-state width is σx=√(ℏ/2mω), and its momentum width is σp=√(mℏω/2). A simple shift changes the mean position but initially changes neither width. During harmonic evolution, this coherent packet remains minimum uncertainty:

σx(t) = √(ℏ/2mω),   σp(t) = √(mℏω/2),   σxσp = ℏ/2.

So the statement “only the ground state reaches ℏ/2” is true among the energy eigenstates |n⟩. It is not a claim about every possible state. Coherent states also reach the bound, even though a shifted one is a superposition and is moving.

Remember

One sentence to keep

A shifted ground-state Gaussian is a moving coherent state: the center oscillates because its energy components change relative phase, while its position and momentum spreads remain at the ground-state values.
